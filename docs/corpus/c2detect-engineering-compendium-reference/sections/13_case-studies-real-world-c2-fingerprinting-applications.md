At the core of the SolarWinds compromise was the insertion of a malicious payload into the SolarWinds Orion server software, which was then distributed to customers via routine software updates. This payload, known as SUNBURST, allowed attackers to establish initial access and exfiltrate data from affected systems. Once the initial foothold was secured, the attackers deployed Cobalt Strike as a C2 platform to maintain control over compromised endpoints. The decision to use Cobalt Strike in this context was deliberate, reflecting its well-established capabilities in establishing and managing long-term command-and-control channels.

In the SolarWinds case, the attackers configured Cobalt Strike to use a custom HTTP/HTTPS protocol that mimicked legitimate traffic patterns, making it difficult for defenders to distinguish between benign and malicious network activity. The use of Cobalt Strike in the SolarWinds campaign also demonstrated its adaptability to different environments and attack vectors. The SolarWinds campaign also highlighted the importance of Cobalt Strike’s ability to evade detection by security tools and network monitoring systems. The success of the SolarWinds campaign also demonstrated the importance of Cobalt Strike’s ability to integrate with other tools and platforms within the attackers' toolkit.
